Social Insurance & GESY in Cyprus (2026)

Two contributions come out of every Cyprus salary alongside income tax. Here are the 2026 rates and the caps that limit them.

Employee contribution rates

ContributionRateAnnual cap
Social Insurance8.8%€68,904
GESY (GHS / ΓεΣΥ)2.65%€180,000

Social Insurance is charged on your gross earnings up to the maximum insurable earnings ceiling of €68,904 for 2026 (up from €62,868 in 2025). Earnings above the ceiling are not subject to further Social Insurance. GESY is charged up to €180,000 of income.

They reduce your income tax

Both contributions are tax-deductible. Income tax is calculated on your gross minus Social Insurance and GESY, so paying them lowers your taxable income. Together they come to 11.45% of gross (below the ceilings), which is comfortably within the deduction limits.

What the employer also pays

Employers pay their own share on top of your salary — Social Insurance of 8.8%, GESY of 2.90%, plus contributions to other state funds (Redundancy, Training, Social Cohesion) totalling roughly 4.7%. This is why the true cost of employing someone is noticeably higher than their gross salary. The calculator can show the full employer cost.

Self-employed differences

Self-employed people pay Social Insurance at 16.6% (on notional income bands set by the state) and GESY at 4.00%. See our self-employed guide for detail.
