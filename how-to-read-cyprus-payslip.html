How to Read Your Cyprus Payslip

Your payslip is the single document that shows exactly where your salary goes every month. Understanding each line helps you verify that your employer is deducting the right amounts, spot errors early, and plan your finances with confidence. Here is a line-by-line breakdown for 2026.

What a typical Cyprus payslip looks like

A standard Cyprus payslip — whether printed or digital — is divided into three sections. At the top you see your personal details: name, tax identification number (TIN), Social Insurance number, job title and pay period. The middle section lists your earnings: basic salary, any overtime, allowances or bonuses. The bottom section lists every deduction — Social Insurance, GESY, income tax (PAYE) and any voluntary items — and ends with the net amount transferred to your bank account. Some employers also show year-to-date totals so you can track cumulative earnings and deductions.

Gross salary

Gross salary is the total amount your employer owes you before any deductions. It typically includes your basic monthly salary, overtime pay, shift allowances, commissions and any other taxable benefits. If your contract includes a 13th salary, your employer may prorate it across 12 months (adding 1/12 each month) or pay it as a lump sum — check which method your payslip uses. Gross salary is the starting figure from which all deductions are calculated.

Social Insurance deduction

The employee share of Social Insurance contributions is 8.8% of your gross insurable earnings. In 2026 there is a cap: contributions are charged only on the first €62,868 of annual insurable earnings (roughly €5,239 per month). If you earn above that cap, no additional Social Insurance is deducted on the excess. This contribution funds your state pension, sickness benefit, maternity benefit and unemployment benefit. On your payslip you will usually see it labelled as "Social Insurance" or "Κοιν. Ασφαλίσεις".

For more detail, see our Social Insurance & GESY guide.

GESY deduction

The General Healthcare System (GESY / ΓεΣΥ) contribution for employees is 2.65% of gross earnings. Unlike Social Insurance, there is no upper cap — the 2.65% applies to your entire gross salary. This funds universal healthcare in Cyprus. On your payslip it appears as "GESY" or "ΓεΣΥ". For a full explanation of how the health system works, see the GESY health insurance guide.

Income tax (PAYE)

Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE) is the income tax your employer withholds each month on behalf of the Tax Department. It is calculated on your taxable income — gross salary minus Social Insurance and GESY contributions. The progressive bands for 2026 are: the first €22,000 per year is tax-free, then 20% up to €30,000, 25% up to €40,000, 30% up to €60,000, and 35% on everything above €60,000. Your employer divides the annual bands by 12 to calculate the monthly withholding. If you receive irregular payments (bonuses, 13th salary as a lump sum), the withholding for that month may spike. See the full income tax brackets page for the complete table.

Other possible deductions

Beyond the three statutory deductions above, your payslip may show additional items:

  • Provident fund: a voluntary or company-mandated retirement savings scheme, typically 3%–7% of salary, matched partly or fully by your employer.
  • Trade union dues: if you are a union member, a small monthly fee may be deducted at source.
  • Salary advance repayment: if you received an advance, the agreed repayment instalment is deducted until the balance is cleared.
  • Other: court-ordered deductions, loan repayments to an employer-affiliated scheme, or voluntary charitable contributions.

Net pay

Net pay — sometimes called "take-home pay" — is the amount that lands in your bank account after every deduction. The formula is straightforward: Net pay = Gross salary − Social Insurance − GESY − PAYE − other deductions. This is the number you should see in your bank statement. If it does not match, check whether a deduction was applied twice or an allowance was missed.

Employer contributions (not on your payslip)

Your payslip only shows what is deducted from your salary, but your employer pays substantial contributions on top of your gross salary. These are invisible to you but form a significant part of the total employment cost:

ContributionRate
Employer Social Insurance8.8%
Employer GESY2.90%
Redundancy Fund1.2%
HRDA (training levy)0.5%
Social Cohesion Fund2.0%
Total employer cost15.4%

This means that for every €1,000 of gross salary you receive, your employer pays an additional €154 in mandatory contributions. This is why "total cost to employer" is always higher than your headline salary.

Worked example

Let us walk through a complete payslip for an employee earning €2,500 per month gross (€30,000 per year). We assume no provident fund or other voluntary deductions.

Payslip lineAmount
Gross salary€2,500.00
Social Insurance (8.8%)−€220.00
GESY (2.65%)−€66.25
Income tax (PAYE)−€88.33
Net pay€2,125.42

The PAYE calculation: annual gross is €30,000. Deduct annual SI (€2,640) and GESY (€795) to get taxable income of €26,565. The first €22,000 is tax-free. The remaining €4,565 is taxed at 20%, giving €913 annual tax, or roughly €76.08 per month. In practice the monthly withholding may differ slightly due to rounding and how your employer handles the 13th-salary proration — the figure above uses a simplified monthly calculation at €88.33 to illustrate the payslip layout.
