Employer costs in Cyprus — the full picture (2026)

When you hire someone in Cyprus, the gross salary on their contract is not your total cost. On top of that gross, the employer pays mandatory contributions totalling roughly 14.9%. This guide breaks down each fund, its rate, its cap and what it actually pays for.

What each fund pays for

Social Insurance (8.3%)

This is the employer's share of the Social Insurance contribution. The employee also pays 8.8% of their own salary. Together, these fund state pensions, sickness and maternity benefits, unemployment benefits, and work-injury benefits. The cap is €68,904 of insurable earnings per year — earnings above that are not subject to Social Insurance.

Redundancy Fund (1.2%)

The Redundancy Fund pays statutory redundancy (severance) payments to employees who are dismissed due to redundancy. The employer contribution is 1.2% of insurable earnings (same cap as Social Insurance). There is no employee contribution to this fund — it is entirely employer-funded.

Industrial Training Fund — HRDA (0.5%)

The Human Resource Development Authority (HRDA / ΑνΑΔ) levy funds vocational training programmes in Cyprus. Employers pay 0.5% of insurable earnings. In return, employers can apply for HRDA subsidies to co-fund training and development programmes for their staff.

Social Cohesion Fund (2.0%)

Introduced in 2019, the Social Cohesion Fund finances social policy measures including the guaranteed minimum income (GMI) scheme. The employer pays 2.0% of total employee emoluments. Unlike the other funds, it does not have the same insurable-earnings cap — it applies to total emoluments.

GESY / GHS — General Healthcare System (2.9%)

The employer's GESY contribution is 2.9% of emoluments, capped at €180,000 per year. This funds Cyprus's universal healthcare system. The employee also contributes 2.65% of their own salary. Self-employed persons, pensioners and the state also contribute to the system.

What the employee pays (for comparison)

For reference, the employee's own deductions are:

  • Social Insurance: 8.8% (capped at €68,904)
  • GESY: 2.65% (capped at €180,000)
  • Income tax: progressive bands from 0% to 35% (on taxable income after SI and GESY)

The employee does not pay into the redundancy fund, industrial training fund or social cohesion fund — those are employer-only costs.

Other costs to consider

Beyond the mandatory contributions above, employers should budget for:

  • Annual leave — minimum 20 working days per year (for a 5-day week), plus public holidays.
  • 13th / 14th salary — if the employment contract or collective agreement provides for it, this is an additional month (or two) of pay.
  • Provident fund / pension — many employers contribute to a provident fund or occupational pension scheme, typically 5–10% of gross salary (this is contractual, not statutory).
  • Professional indemnity or liability insurance — depending on the industry.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost an employer to hire someone in Cyprus?

On top of the gross salary, the employer pays approximately 14.9% in mandatory contributions (Social Insurance, redundancy, training, social cohesion and GESY). An employee earning €30,000 gross costs the employer roughly €34,470 in total.

What is the employer's Social Insurance rate?

The employer's Social Insurance rate is 8.3% of insurable earnings, capped at €68,904 per year. This is separate from the employee's own 8.8% contribution.

Are employer contributions capped?

Social Insurance, redundancy and training are capped at the insurable earnings ceiling (€68,904/year). GESY is capped at €180,000. The social cohesion fund has no published cap.

Do employers pay income tax on behalf of employees?

No. Income tax (PAYE) is withheld from the employee's salary and remitted to the tax department, but it is the employee's tax — not an additional employer cost.

Is the 13th salary an employer cost?

If the employment contract or collective agreement provides for a 13th or 14th salary, then yes — it is an additional month of gross pay plus the employer contributions on that amount. It is not a statutory requirement for all employers.

Note: Rates and caps change. Always confirm the current employer contribution rates with the Social Insurance Services or a qualified accountant before budgeting.
