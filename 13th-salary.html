The 13th and 14th salary in Cyprus

Many Cypriot employers pay an extra month's salary at the end of the year — and sometimes a 14th before summer. Here's how it works.

What is the 13th salary?

The 13th salary is an additional payment, usually equal to one month's gross, paid around December. It is a widespread practice in Cyprus, often written into employment contracts, though whether it is guaranteed depends on your specific contract or collective agreement. A 14th salary — a second bonus month — exists in some sectors and is typically paid before the summer holidays.

How your annual pay is split

If your annual gross is fixed, being paid over 13 instead of 12 months simply means each monthly payment is smaller, with the extra cheque in December. If your monthly figure is fixed and a 13th is added on top, your annual total is higher. The calculator lets you choose 12, 13 or 14 payments so you can see both views.

Is the 13th salary taxed?

Yes. The 13th (and 14th) salary is part of your taxable employment income. Cyprus income tax is progressive and calculated on your total annual income, so the bonus month is taxed at your marginal rate along with the rest. Social Insurance and GESY also apply, subject to the annual caps. Because tax is worked out on the yearly total, splitting it across 12 or 13 payments does not change the total tax you pay — only the size of each pay cheque.

Quick example

On a €26,000 annual gross paid over 13 months, each payment is €2,000 gross. The annual tax, Social Insurance and GESY are the same as if it were paid over 12 months of €2,166.67 — the calculator computes the yearly totals and then divides the net across your chosen number of payments.
