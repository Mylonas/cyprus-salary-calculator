Cyprus minimum wage — rates, rules & history

Cyprus introduced its first national minimum wage on 1 January 2024. Here is what you need to know: the current rate, who it covers, exemptions, and how it compares to the rest of the EU.

When the minimum wage was introduced in January 2024, new employees received a slightly lower rate of €940/month during their first six months of employment. After those six months — or from 1 July 2024 for employees already in post — the rate rises to €1,000/month. This €1,000 rate continues to apply in 2025 and 2026 unless the government revises it.

The figure is the gross monthly salary. After Social Insurance (8.8%), GESY (2.65%) and income tax, the net take-home is lower. On a €1,000 gross with 12 salaries and no pension, the net is roughly €885/month — use the salary calculator for an exact breakdown.

Who does it apply to?

The minimum wage applies to all employees in Cyprus — Cypriot citizens and foreign nationals alike, full-time and part-time (pro-rated for part-timers). It covers every sector of the economy with a few exceptions:

  • Domestic workers — live-in domestic helpers have a separate, lower statutory minimum set by decree (currently around €460/month plus board and lodging).
  • Agricultural and maritime workers — employees covered by specific collective agreements in agriculture and shipping may have different minimum terms.
  • Trainees and apprentices — participants in recognised government apprenticeship schemes may receive stipends below the minimum wage during the training period.

For part-time employees, the minimum is calculated proportionally. If the standard full-time week is 40 hours and you work 20 hours, your minimum monthly gross is €500.

History — how Cyprus got a minimum wage

Before 2024, Cyprus was one of the few EU member states with no national minimum wage. Instead, it had a system of sector-specific minimum wages (Minimum Guaranteed Wages) that applied only to nine named occupations — shop assistants, clerks, childcare workers, nursing assistants, school attendants, security guards, personal care workers, cleaners and caretakers. Their rate was €924/month after six months of service.

In November 2022, the EU adopted the Directive on Adequate Minimum Wages (2022/2041), which required member states to either have a statutory minimum wage or demonstrate that collective bargaining covers at least 80% of workers. Cyprus, with low collective bargaining coverage, chose to legislate. The Council of Ministers approved the new minimum wage decree in December 2023, effective 1 January 2024.

What does a minimum-wage worker actually take home?

On a gross of €1,000/month (€12,000/year) with 12 salaries:

  • Social Insurance (8.8%): €88/month
  • GESY (2.65%): €26.50/month
  • Income tax: €0 (annual gross of €12,000 is well below the €22,000 tax-free threshold)
  • Net take-home: roughly €885/month

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum wage in Cyprus in 2026?

The national minimum gross wage is €1,000 per month (€6.25/hour for a 40-hour week). This rate took effect on 1 January 2024 and remains in force for 2026.

Does the minimum wage apply to all workers?

It applies to all employees in Cyprus regardless of nationality or sector, with limited exemptions for domestic workers (who have a separate lower minimum), and for agricultural and maritime workers covered by specific collective agreements.

Is the €1,000 figure gross or net?

It is gross. After Social Insurance (8.8%), GESY (2.65%) and income tax, the net take-home is lower — roughly €885/month on €1,000 gross.

Can an employer pay less than the minimum wage?

No. Paying below the minimum wage is a criminal offence under the decree. Employees can report violations to the Department of Labour Relations. The only exceptions are the specific categories listed above (domestic workers, recognised apprenticeships, certain agricultural/maritime collective agreements).

Will the minimum wage increase?

The government can revise the minimum wage by decree. The EU directive requires member states to review adequacy regularly. Any future increase will depend on economic conditions, inflation and political decisions — check the Ministry of Labour for the latest.

Note: Rates and rules change. Always confirm the current minimum wage and its applicability with the Ministry of Labour, Welfare and Social Insurance or your employer before relying on a figure.
