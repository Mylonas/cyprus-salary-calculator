Salary Negotiation in Cyprus: What to Know

Whether you are starting a new job or asking for a raise, understanding the local market is key to negotiating well in Cyprus. This guide covers salary ranges, the 13th salary factor, benefits, and practical tips for 2026.

Salaries in Cyprus vary widely by industry, experience and location. The table below shows approximate gross annual ranges for mid-level professionals in 2026. Senior roles and management positions often exceed the upper end.

The 13th salary factor

Many private-sector employers in Cyprus pay a 13th salary — an extra month's pay, usually split between summer and Christmas. Some also offer a 14th salary. This effectively adds 8.3% (or 16.7% for a 14th) to your annual compensation. When evaluating an offer, always ask whether the quoted salary includes the 13th salary or is on top of it. A "€24,000 per year" offer might actually mean €26,000 total if the 13th salary is additional. Learn more in our 13th and 14th salary guide.

Benefits beyond salary

In Cyprus, the total compensation package often matters more than the base salary alone. Common benefits to negotiate include:

  • Private health insurance — covers private hospitals and specialists beyond the public GESY system. Often extended to family members.
  • Provident fund — employer contributions to a retirement savings fund, typically 5–10% of salary. This is essentially free money toward your future.
  • Meal vouchers — tax-efficient food allowances used at supermarkets and restaurants, worth €100–€150 per month at many companies.
  • Company car — common in sales, management and field roles. Saves €300–€500+ per month in lease and fuel costs.
  • Remote work allowance — increasingly offered post-pandemic, covering internet, equipment or co-working space costs.
  • Relocation package — for expats, may include flight tickets, temporary housing, visa support and a settling-in allowance.

Gross vs net: what actually matters

A salary figure means little until you know what lands in your bank account each month. Cyprus deducts income tax (progressive bands starting after €22,000), Social Insurance (8.8%), GESY (2.65%), and small contributions for the redundancy and industrial training funds. A €30,000 gross offer does not net €30,000 — use the calculator to see the real take-home. When comparing offers, always compare net figures, not gross.

Cost of living context

Where you live in Cyprus significantly affects how far your salary goes. Limassol is the most expensive city, with rents 30–50% higher than the national average, driven by the tech and financial services boom. Nicosia is more moderate, while Larnaca and Paphos are the most affordable major cities. Factor in housing, transport and daily costs when evaluating offers — a €35,000 salary in Paphos may leave you with more disposable income than €40,000 in Limassol. Negotiation tips for Cyprus

The Cypriot job market has its own conventions. Here are practical tips:

  • Research market rates — use job postings, the salary table above, and our calculator to anchor your expectations in data, not guesswork.
  • Negotiate the total package — base salary is only part of the picture. A provident fund, health insurance and meal vouchers can add thousands in effective value.
  • Ask about the 13th salary — confirm whether the offer includes it. If it does not, the actual annual total is higher than the headline number.
  • Consider expat tax benefits — if you are relocating to Cyprus, you may qualify for the 50% income tax exemption on earnings above €55,000 under the non-domicile rules. This dramatically changes net pay. See our expat tax relief guide.
  • Be specific, not vague — state a clear number or range backed by market data rather than saying "I want more."
  • Get it in writing — verbal promises about bonuses, reviews or benefits should appear in the employment contract or a signed offer letter.

When to negotiate

Timing matters in salary negotiation. The best moments are: when you receive a job offer (before signing), at the end of your probation period (typically 6 months), during annual performance reviews, or when you take on significantly more responsibility. If the market is tight in your field — particularly IT, fintech and compliance in 2026 — you have more leverage. Avoid negotiating during company-wide cost-cutting or immediately after joining.

Minimum wage floor

Since January 2024, Cyprus has a national minimum wage of €1,000 gross per month (€885 per month for the first six months of employment). This applies across all sectors. While skilled professionals earn well above this floor, it is useful context: any offer below the minimum is illegal, and the minimum sets a baseline that affects entry-level positions across hospitality, retail and agriculture.
