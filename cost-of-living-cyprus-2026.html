Cost of Living in Cyprus 2026

Cyprus offers a Mediterranean lifestyle at costs well below Western Europe — but prices have been climbing steadily. Here is what you can realistically expect to spend each month in 2026, whether you are relocating or already settled on the island.

Rent

Rent is the single biggest line in most budgets. Prices vary sharply by city and neighbourhood. Limassol is the most expensive market, driven by the tech sector and expat demand; Nicosia sits in the middle; Larnaca and Paphos remain more affordable. Expect to pay roughly 40-60% more for a two-bedroom apartment compared with a one-bedroom in the same area.

These ranges cover central and near-central locations. Moving further out, or choosing an older building without a lift, can bring the rent down noticeably. Most landlords require one or two months' deposit up front.

Utilities

Electricity is the largest utility bill and fluctuates with the season. Air conditioning pushes summer bills well above winter ones. The Electricity Authority of Cyprus (EAC) is the sole provider; there is no market choice. Water is billed separately by each municipality.

Groceries

A single person spending reasonably can expect a monthly grocery bill of €250–€400, depending on diet and shopping habits. Local produce — fruit, vegetables, halloumi, olive oil — is good value; imported and packaged goods cost more. The main supermarket chains are Alphamega, Papantoniou, Sklavenitis, and Metro, with discount chain Lidl expanding across the island.

Shopping at open-air markets (laiki agora) on Wednesdays and Saturdays is a reliable way to bring the fruit and vegetable bill down by 20–30% compared with supermarket prices.

Transport

Cyprus has no metro, tram, or rail network. Public transport is limited to buses, operated mainly by OSEL in Nicosia, EMEL in Limassol, and Zenon in Larnaca and Paphos. A single bus ride costs €1.50 and a monthly pass around €40. Intercity buses connect the main cities for €4–€7 per trip.

In practice, most residents drive. Fuel costs roughly €1.45 per litre (petrol) in mid-2026. Annual car insurance ranges from €300 for basic third-party to €800 or more for comprehensive cover on a newer vehicle. Road tax, annual MOT, and maintenance add another €400–€700 per year. If you are on a tight budget, a used car plus insurance and fuel will still be cheaper than relying on taxis for daily commuting.

Dining out and entertainment

Eating out in Cyprus is affordable compared with most of the EU. A casual meal at a local taverna — souvlaki, kebab, or a meze plate — costs €10–€15 per person. A sit-down dinner at a mid-range restaurant, with a main course, salad, and a drink, runs €25–€40. Coffee culture is strong: a freddo cappuccino at a café costs €3–€4, and a local beer €3.50–€5.

Cinema tickets are around €8–€10. A gym membership runs €35–€55 per month. Nightlife is concentrated in Limassol and Ayia Napa, with cocktail bar prices similar to other Southern European cities at €8–€12 per drink.

Healthcare

Cyprus operates a universal healthcare system called GESY (GHS). Every employee and self-employed person contributes a percentage of income, and in return has access to GPs, specialists, hospital care, and prescription medicines through the system. GESY has significantly reduced the out-of-pocket burden for residents since its launch.

Some residents also carry private health insurance — typically €50–€150 per month — for faster access to specialists, private hospital rooms, or coverage abroad. Private insurance is not required but remains popular, especially among expats accustomed to private-sector healthcare.

How salary compares to costs

The minimum wage in Cyprus is €1,000 gross per month (after the six-month probation rate of €940). After tax and social contributions, that leaves roughly €885 net — enough to cover basic living expenses in a shared flat outside Limassol, but tight for a single person renting alone.

A gross salary of €2,000 per month yields approximately €1,650 net. At €3,000 gross the take-home is around €2,350. Use the salary calculator to see exact deductions for any salary level. The comfort threshold for a single person renting alone is generally around €1,500–€1,800 net, depending on the city.

Salaries have been rising — especially in tech, finance, and professional services — but rent increases in Limassol have outpaced wage growth for several years. Salary negotiation matters more than ever: a €200 difference in monthly net income can shift your budget from tight to comfortable.
